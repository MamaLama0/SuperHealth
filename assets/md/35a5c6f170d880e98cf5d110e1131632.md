## Setting up
1. Place a dumbbell (or an alternative weight) on the ground and lie down on your side with the weight in front of your abdomen.
    1. A dumbbell is most commonly used for this exercise, but you can alternatively select a weight plate, ankle weight, or kettlebell. Of the four options, a kettlebell can be the most awkward weight to hold on your thigh, so it is the least recommended weight.
    2. You also have the option of using a weighted vest for resistance, which you would don before lying down, and then ignore the instructions about holding a weight by your side.
    3. If you are on a hard surface, you can set up on an exercise mat for comfort. You chiefly want a comfortable surface under the forearm that you will place on the ground.
    4. The muscles on the side closest to the ground are predominantly trained during your side plank.
2. Straighten and align your legs with your torso so that you can draw a straight line from your legs through your hips to your shoulders when viewed from above.
    1. Your top leg should be resting on your bottom leg with the inner portions of your feet touching.
3. Position your bottom forearm on the ground with your elbow bent to about 90 degrees, as it is close to being stacked directly underneath your shoulder.
    1. With your bottom hand, you can make a fist and keep the little finger-side of your hand in contact with the ground. Alternatively, you can keep your palm flat on the ground if you find that to feel more comfortable and stable.
    2. Your upper arm closer to the ground should be in a vertical position.
    3. You can perform this step simultaneously with step 2.
4. Using your top arm, pick up the weight, and position your arm by your top side with your palm facing down. Based on your preference, you can rest the weight on the side of your thigh with a straight elbow, or on the side of your hip with a mildly bent elbow.
## Exercise technique
1. Lift your bottom hip off the ground by pushing through your bottom foot and forearm. You should now be able to draw a straight line from your head through your belly button to the center point between your feet when viewed from the front.
    1. You should also be able to draw a straight line from your heel through your hip to your shoulder when viewed from the top.
    2. Your legs, torso, and head should be in the same alignment as they would be when standing upright with your feet together.
    3. Continue using your top arm to keep the weight stable against the side of your thigh/hip throughout the set.
2. Statically hold this position in proper alignment until you have completed your set and are ready to repeat the exercise with your opposite side contacting the ground.
    1. Avoid letting your lower hip sag, which would prevent you from drawing a line through the midline of your body when viewed from the front. If you notice your hip starting to drop down, it can help to think about pushing more forcefully through your bottom foot. When learning this exercise, it can be useful to face a mirror to receive visual feedback if your lower hip begins sagging down.
    2. Avoid letting your buttocks drift backward by bending your hips. This would prevent you from drawing a straight line through your whole body when viewed from above. Squeezing your glutes can help prevent you from unintentionally bending your hips.
## Description
This exercise can statically train your [gluteus medius](https://www.notion.so/Gluteus-Medius-1925c6f170d880f38e56e316eab6e33b?pvs=21), [gluteus minimus](https://www.notion.so/Gluteus-Minimus-1925c6f170d880468a1ac852b36eeaf8?pvs=21), [lateral deltoid](https://www.notion.so/Deltoid-Lateral-Head-1905c6f170d8804b9b6ef12253eec774?pvs=21), and a variety of core muscles, including your [obliques](https://www.notion.so/Abdominal-Obliques-1b95c6f170d880f5bdc1c2ca4cd25d5f?pvs=21) and [quadratus lumborum](https://www.notion.so/Quadratus-Lumborum-1965c6f170d880b89d4fc43238472b46?pvs=21).
